6-Month Outlook · Global Context
Crude oil shock from Hormuz closure is the #1 FMCG cost risk — input basket now net negative
▼ Brent $97 — +48% YoY · Hormuz spike ▼ Palm oil +17% YoY — double pressure ▲ Wheat/sugar at multi-yr lows → EIA forecasts easing to $89 in Q4 FY26 → INR range-bound 83–87
The input cost basket has flipped from net positive to net negative for Indian FMCG following the Strait of Hormuz shock. Brent averaged $117/bbl in April 2026 (peak $138 on Apr 7) after US-Iran conflict effectively closed the strait — this is the highest since June 2022. Even at the current ~$97/bbl, packaging costs (PET, HDPE, laminate films) and logistics will pressure FMCG gross margins by 150–250bps in Q1 FY27. Combined with palm oil at +17% YoY, companies like HUL, Marico, and Godrej CP face their worst raw material environment since 2022. EIA STEO (May 2026) forecasts: Brent easing to ~$106 in May–Jun, $89 in Q4 FY26, $79 in 2027 as Hormuz flows resume. Relief: Wheat and sugar remain at multi-year lows, providing partial offset for food FMCG.
